# EDA Findings

Generated by `src/eda.py` from `data/processed/combined_hourly.csv`
(pre-cleaning; NaNs skipped by pandas aggregation, not imputed). Figures
in `reports/figures/`.

## Headline numbers

- 90,542 hourly floor-rows, 2018-07-01 to 2019-12-31. 1.8% of rows have
  NaN energy (fully-missing hours; see `reports/data_inspection.md` for
  why, and step 5 for how they'll be handled).
- Mean hourly energy is strongly occupancy-driven: **weekday mean 42.3
  vs weekend mean 19.6** (combined, ~2.2x).
- Combined peak hour is **08:00**, trough is **03:00**.
- Floors 2-7 sit in a similar band (mean hourly energy ~21-29). **Floor 1
  is a clear outlier at ~106** — see below.

## Daily / seasonal (figs 01, 02, 05)

- `02_per_floor_daily_energy.png` shows the full 18-month span per floor.
  Floors 2-7 show a consistent, repeating workday sawtooth. Floor 1 sits
  on a much higher, noisier band throughout, with no sharp seasonal
  trend break at the 2018->2019 boundary.
- Floor 6's series visibly stops in **late October 2019**, matching the
  file truncation documented in `data_inspection.md` — expected, not a
  new finding, but visually confirms it.
- No obvious multi-month seasonal trend (e.g. hot-season AC surge) jumps
  out at a glance in the daily aggregate for floors 2-7; `05_monthly_seasonal.png`
  gives a smoother monthly view for closer inspection, and step 6
  (seasonal decomposition) will quantify this properly rather than
  eyeballing it.

## Weekly / daily profile (figs 03, 04)

- `03_hourly_profile_weekday_weekend.png` is the clearest signal in the
  dataset: floors 2-7 all show a textbook office pattern — near-zero
  overnight, a sharp ramp around 07:00-08:00, a **midday dip around
  12:00-13:00** (lunch break, visible as a consistent notch on every one
  of those floors), a second peak in the early afternoon, and a ramp-down
  after ~17:00-18:00. Weekend curves for these floors are flat and low.
- **Floor 1 does not follow this pattern.** Its weekday and weekend
  curves are close together, its trough is mid-morning (not overnight),
  and it has a pronounced peak in the **evening (~18:00-20:00)** rather
  than midday. This points to Floor 1 running different load types than
  the others (e.g., a large always-on system plus an evening-driven
  load) rather than standard daytime office occupancy — worth carrying
  into the model-interpretation step (12) as a floor-specific note, and
  worth double-checking it isn't a units/aggregation artifact before
  trusting it (Floor 1's raw schema has only 11 kW columns including one
  very large AC channel, `z2_AC1(kW)`, seen up to ~45 kW in the raw
  sample — consistent with a big central unit, not obviously an error).
- `04_day_of_week.png` confirms Mon-Fri >> Sat-Sun for floors 2-7, with
  Floor 1 again the least sensitive to day-of-week.

## Distribution (fig 06)

- `06_energy_distribution_per_floor.png` (outliers hidden) shows floors
  2-7 with broadly similar median/IQR and Floor 1 offset well above the
  rest, consistent with the mean numbers above.

## Implications for later steps

- `floor_id` as a categorical feature is clearly justified — floors are
  not interchangeable, both in scale (Floor 1) and in temporal shape
  (Floor 1's inverted daily profile).
- Hour-of-day, day-of-week/weekend, and month are all going to be useful
  engineered features (step 7) given how strong these patterns are.
- The Floor 6 truncation means step 10 (stability across floors/months)
  should expect **no Floor 6 rows for Nov-Dec 2019** and report that
  explicitly rather than showing a misleading gap or an averaged-away
  score.
